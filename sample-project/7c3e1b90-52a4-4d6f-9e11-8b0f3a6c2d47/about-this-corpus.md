# About this corpus

A constructed corpus, made to show what this system does with prose. The Cape Aldis expedition did not happen: there was no such shore party, no *Nimbus*, and none of the six men existed. Every entry here was written for this project.

It is shaped like the real thing on purpose. A shore party of six wintering at about 78 degrees south in 1912 would keep a hut journal in rotation with the night watch, which is why the hand changes with every entry and why no two of them write alike. The sun sets at that latitude in the last days of April and returns in the third week of August, and the documents follow that calendar, because the calendar is what gives the figures in [findings.md](findings.md) their shape.

Read one month and you have the corpus. March is the party settling in, June is midwinter, August is the light coming back, October is the depot party leaving. The rest is the same six men in different weather.

## What is here

Eight monthly journal files, March to October 1912. One private notebook kept by the leader across the same season, which is the same events written for nobody, and the most useful document in the corpus for exactly that reason.

Seven codes in two files, and a third file holding the rules that govern all of them. The codes are deliberately interpretive: four of the seven cannot be found by searching for words, only by reading. That is the point of putting a model on a corpus rather than a query.

[findings.md](findings.md) carries three figures and the argument they support, including one figure that shows a clean correlation the prose then refuses to use.
