# Hut journal — September 1912

**2 September.** Sun three hours twelve. Barometer 29.33, wind SE force 3, minimum −38. Everything moderate, and every man in the hut behaving as though the season had already broken. I record the figures because they are the figures, but I notice I am reading them to people who want them to say something they do not say. — E.P.B.

**6 September.** The depot party is Thorsen, Leggat and myself, and I have told them so, and I have not yet told Culshaw what it does to his stores. That is the wrong order and I knew it when I did it. — G.R.M.

**9 September.** Told at last, and by Thorsen, not by Mowbray. Forty days for three men. I make it fifty-two pounds of what is in the annexe including nine of the sugar, and that is before the dogs. Sugar stands at fifty-four. So we come out of the winter with the margin I have been protecting for six months spent in one line of a list. It may be the right line. I would have liked to be in the room. — H.C.

**11 September.** Wind SE force 6 with drift, cleared by evening. Minimum −34, which is high for the date. I have been asked three times today whether it is a good sign. It is one reading. I have said so three times and been believed none of them. — E.P.B.

**13 September.** Trial trip, eleven miles out and back with a light load. The surface is exactly what Thorsen said it would be in July and nothing like what the plan assumed. We will take four days to do what the plan does in three, every time, all the way out. This has been reported. The plan has not changed. — J.L.

**17 September.** Two of the party want the depot further out and one wants it nearer, and the argument has been running four days now dressed as an argument about dog feed. I have let it run because I would rather it came out here than at sixty miles. — G.R.M.

**21 September.** Equinox. Barrow marked it by reading the two columns of his table aloud, the sun and the twilight, from March round to now, and it took some time and nobody interrupted him. I did not expect to be moved by a list of figures and was. — W.S.

**25 September.** Wind three days, drift, nothing to be done, and the party sat in the hut with the sledges packed and looked at them. Bad temper of a new kind — in July nobody could go, and now everybody could go but for this. — J.L.

**27 September.** Everything is packed and everything is argued about. Leggat and Thorsen have not agreed on a single item of the loading and have agreed on the whole load, which I take to mean that the disagreement was never about the load. Mowbray leaves them to it, which is either wisdom or the appearance of it. — W.S.

**29 September.** Loads final. Two hundred and ten pounds a sledge which is over what Thorsen wanted and under what the plan wanted, so it is nobody's number and everybody has agreed to it. — H.C.
