# Hut journal — June 1912

**5 June.** Barometer 28.94 and falling since noon. Wind SSE, force 6, drift at the anemometer mast, which is the third time this month I have gone out to free it and the third time I have come back with the impression that it would rather not be freed. Minimum overnight −47. The screen is doing its work but the ink freezes in the recorder, so I am reduced to reading and writing figures by hand, which introduces precisely the error the recorder exists to remove. — E.P.B.

**11 June.** Rehearsal, if that is the word, for the dinner. Culshaw will not say what is in it. Barrow has printed the menus on the hand press and will not let them be seen. Mowbray has written a speech, which he denies. We are eight days out and the entire hut is keeping secrets from itself, and I have not seen the men better. — W.S.

**14 June.** Twilight at noon nil for the eighth day. I have kept the column anyway with a stroke in it, and Sparrow asked me why I bother recording a quantity that is zero and will be zero tomorrow. Because it will not be zero in August, and the day it stops being zero I should like to have the day before it. — E.P.B.

**19 June.** Midwinter. Culshaw has been at the stove since six and would not let anyone near it, and the menu Barrow printed runs to nine courses, each named for a place none of us has seen since March. Consommé Aldis. Petrel à la Rennell. It is absurd, it took four days, and I do not think a man here would have given it up for anything. What interests me is that nobody proposed it. It became inevitable somewhere in the second week of May and the labour attached itself to whoever stood nearest. Mowbray made a short speech and sat down before he had finished it, which I thought the most sensible thing done all evening. Thorsen sang. I have no idea what about. — W.S.

**24 June.** Blowing very hard all day and the hut cracking like a ship. Wind got in under the eves on the sowth side and I have wedged it with a bit of packing case wich will hold I think. Nobody could go out. The stove will not draw proper when it blows like this and Culshaw says it is the stove but it is not the stove, it is him shutting the damper down to save fuel and then wondering why. I have said nothing. Ice on the inside of the wall by my bunk two fingers thick. Scraped it off. It come back. — J.L.

**28 June.** Went through the pemmican cases with Mowbray. Fourteen intact, two we have been into, and those two down to about half. That is not the trouble. The trouble is the sugar, which I make sixty-one pounds, and if the depot party takes what the plan says it takes there is not sixty-one pounds' worth of margin between here and the ship, whenever the ship comes. Mowbray says we will settle the allowance when the light returns. I said the sugar will not know it is waiting for the light. He laughed and did not answer, which I have learned means he has already decided and is not telling me until he has told Thorsen. — H.C.
