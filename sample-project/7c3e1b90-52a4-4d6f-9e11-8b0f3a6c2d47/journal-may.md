# Hut journal — May 1912

**2 May.** Barometer 29.05 and unsettled, wind SSE force 4, minimum −29. First entry made entirely by lamp. I find I am writing larger. — E.P.B.

**4 May.** The lamps are the whole business now. Blubber for the stove, oil for the reading, and a rule against both at once in the same corner, which is mine and which nobody keeps. I have moved my bunk to be nearer the lamp and this has been noticed and commented on twice. — J.L.

**9 May.** Twilight at noon is a grey band over the ridge and nothing more. I make it twenty-two minutes. Sparrow says he can read large print by it at the door and I do not believe him, but I have not gone out with him to check, which I ought to do and will. — E.P.B.

**11 May.** Mowbray has begun asking each of us, separately and as though it had just occurred to him, what we think the depot journey should carry. He has asked me twice. I have given the same answer twice and he wrote it down the second time. — H.C.

**14 May.** Sixty hours of wind. There is a particular note it makes across the stove pipe that I would know anywhere now, and when it stops the silence is worse. Two of the dogs got out of the shelter and Thorsen was out for an hour in it, which he then did not mention. — G.R.M.

**19 May.** Somebody has begun printing a bill of fare for midwinter and pinning it up a fortnight at a time, revised. It is Barrow. He denies it. Nine courses at last count and one of them in Latin. Nobody has asked him to stop and nobody will. — W.S.

**23 May.** The stove will not draw when it blows from the south and I have taken the pipe apart twice looking for the fault. There is no fault. It is the wind on the cowl. Culshaw wants the damper set his way regardless and I have set it his way and it draws worse, and I have said nothing about that either. — J.L.

**25 May.** I have been keeping a note of who speaks at meals and it is not a thing I will show anybody, but the shape of it is that four of us talk and two do not, and the two are not the two who were quiet on the ship. That is a change over eight weeks and it is worth watching, and I am aware that watching it is my own way of not being one of the quiet ones. — W.S.

**28 May.** Pemmican into the second case properly now. Sugar sixty-three, which is three pounds in five weeks and better than April, and I have said so, and nobody was interested. Butter is turning at the edges. I will use it before I explain it. — H.C.

**31 May.** Dogs are restless in the dark. They sleep and then they are all awake at once for nothing. It is the same with the men but the men have books. — A.T.
