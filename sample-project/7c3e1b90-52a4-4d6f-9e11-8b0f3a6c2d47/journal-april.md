# Hut journal — April 1912

**3 April.** Sun eight hours forty. Barometer 29.20 and falling all afternoon, wind backing to SE. Minimum −19. The recorder is behaving; the ink is not yet troubled. I have begun a second column for the length of twilight either side, which will matter more than the sun itself before long. — E.P.B.

**6 April.** The dogs do not care about the light going. They care about the wind, and about who feeds them, and about each other, in that order. I say this because two of the men have asked me whether the dogs are troubled by the dark, and both of them asked in a way that was not about dogs. — A.T.

**8 April.** Two days of it now, the wind off the glacier and into the door as though it had been aimed. Every man who comes in brings a hundredweight of snow with him and every man who comes in is told so by Culshaw, and this is now the entire content of our conversation. — J.L.

**11 April.** Mowbray asked me to look at Leggat's hand, which he had cut on the packing case and left three days. It is clean enough. What interests me more is that he did not come to me and would not have come today if Thorsen had not said something. There is a great deal here that will be left three days. — W.S.

**16 April.** Sun four hours ten. It comes up the colour of a bruise and does not clear the ridge to the north, so that we are already living in a kind of permanent afternoon. The men have noticed. Nobody says anything about it directly, and everybody has begun to say what the time is. — G.R.M.

**19 April.** Rehung the annexe door so it opens with the wind and not against it. Took a day. Culshaw says the stores are colder now for it, which is true and is the price, and he has said it four times, which is not the price. — J.L.

**22 April.** Went round the stores with the leader. Sugar sixty-six. The two cases we are into are down by about a third between them and it is not yet May. If we go on at this rate we do not run out, but we run out of the margin, which is a different thing and harder to explain to a man with a mug in his hand. — H.C.

**26 April.** Last of it today. Barrow had us all out at noon and we watched the top edge sit on the ridge for perhaps four minutes and go. There was some cheering, which surprised me. Thorsen shook hands with everybody, which surprised me more. — W.S.

**30 April.** Twilight at noon, forty minutes by my reckoning and shortening fast. Barometer 28.88. Wind SSE force 5, drift at the mast. I record the drift each time now, since it is the one thing that decides whether the instruments are read at all. — E.P.B.
