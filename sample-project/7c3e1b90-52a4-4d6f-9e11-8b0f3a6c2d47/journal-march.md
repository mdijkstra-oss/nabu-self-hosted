# Hut journal — March 1912

**2 March.** The *Nimbus* went out past the north point at four and was gone from the glass by half past five. We stood on the ice foot until we could not pretend to see her. Mowbray then set everyone to the stores, which I think was the right instinct and was resented by every man there. — W.S.

**5 March.** Hut is sound. I have been round the whole of it with a bar and there is nothing wanting except the annexe door, which was hung by somebody in a hurry on the ship and swings the wrong way for a wind out of the south, and a wind out of the south is what we are told to expect. I will rehang it. Nobody has asked me to and nobody will stop me. — J.L.

**7 March.** Screen erected forty yards SSW of the hut, clear of the drift shadow. Barometer 29.61, steady. Wind NE, force 3. Minimum overnight −11. Sun above the horizon eleven hours and a few minutes; I shall keep the figure daily, since it is the one quantity here that does what it is told. — E.P.B.

**13 March.** Dug out the coal and stacked it against the west wall where it is handiest and where, Thorsen tells me, it will be under six feet of drift by June and I will curse myself. He is probably right. He did not offer to help me move it. — J.L.

**16 March.** Sun nine hours fifty and losing better than four minutes a day. It is a curious thing to watch a quantity fall that you know will reach nothing and stay there, and I find myself checking the arithmetic more often than the arithmetic needs. Barometer 29.44, wind NE force 2, minimum −14. — E.P.B.

**19 March.** Blizzard, thirty hours. The hut takes it better than the ship did but the noise is a different thing altogether — on the ship you were being carried and here you are being leaned on. Nobody out. Sparrow lectured on the anatomy of the crabeater to an audience of five, of whom two slept. — G.R.M.

**24 March.** Counted the pemmican into the annexe. Fourteen cases and a broken one, and the broken one has taken damp, which nobody will admit to having stowed. I have set it aside to be eaten first rather than argued about. Sugar sixty-eight pounds. Flour good. Butter I do not trust past September. — H.C.

**27 March.** Sparrow wanted a bench for his microscope and Leggat wanted the same timber for the annexe door and both of them came to me rather than to each other, which tells me more about the winter ahead than either job does. Timber to Leggat. Sparrow has the crate the theodolite came in and appears content, which I do not entirely believe. — G.R.M.

**29 March.** The dogs are well. Snow is right for them now, hard and not yet the sugar it becomes in the cold. I take them out most days. Some of the men do not like that I take the best snow days for the dogs but the dogs do not get better in the hut. — A.T.
