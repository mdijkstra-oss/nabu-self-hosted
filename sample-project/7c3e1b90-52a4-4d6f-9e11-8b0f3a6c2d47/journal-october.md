# Hut journal — October 1912

**2 October.** Sun eight hours forty. Barometer 29.55, wind light NE, drift nil, minimum −24. Conditions for going, and I have said so in those words, which I do not think I have ever done in a met book before and which I am aware is not what a met book is for. — E.P.B.

**5 October.** They went at eight. Three men, two teams, the sledges heavier than agreed and the surface holding. Sparrow and I watched them to the second ridge and then went in and found the hut a different size. — G.R.M.

**7 October.** The hut is quiet enough that I can hear the recorder. Barometer 29.61, wind NE force 2, minimum −21, twenty-one hours of daylight. I have moved the noon reading to eight in the morning because there is no longer any reason to wait for noon and I had not noticed until today that the reason was the dark. — E.P.B.

**9 October.** Quiet. Four of us in a hut built for seven and the noise of it wrong all day. Culshaw has cooked for seven twice now and said nothing about it either time. — W.S.

**11 October.** Sparrow has taken over the dogs with me, which he does not need to do and does badly and which I am glad of. Four men in a hut is worse than seven. Everybody knows where everybody is all the time. — A.T.

**14 October.** Sugar fifty-one, and it will not move much until they are back, and then it will move all at once. I find I am keeping the figure for nobody. I have kept it anyway and put it on the door. — H.C.

**18 October.** Blow from the SE, force 8, thirty hours. It will have caught them somewhere past the depot and there is nothing to be said about that, so we said nothing about it, all day, at some length. — G.R.M.

**23 October.** Barometer 29.48 and rising, wind fallen away, twenty hours of daylight. I have kept the columns since the seventh of March and this is the first entry I have written knowing that the figures are being read by three men out on the barrier as well as by the four of us here, which they are not, and which does not stop it being true. — E.P.B.

**27 October.** Dogs left behind are impossible. They know. I take them out and they pull north and I take them out again and they pull north. — A.T.

**29 October.** Sugar fifty-one still. I have caught myself going in to look at it twice in a day when I know the figure, and I know why, and it is not about the sugar. — H.C.

**31 October.** Nothing from the depot party, which is expected and is not the same as nothing being wrong. Sparrow keeps a light in the north window against the argument that they cannot possibly see it. Nobody has argued. — G.R.M.
