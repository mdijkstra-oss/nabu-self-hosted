# Hut journal — July 1912

**2 July.** The wind has been from the south for nine days and has stopped being weather. It is a condition, like the dark, and we have started to speak of it in the same way — not "it is blowing" but "it is still blowing", as one says of an illness. I do not like what that does to a man's sense of when a thing will end. — W.S.

**6 July.** Minimum −51, the lowest yet. Barometer 29.40 and steady, which is the odd part: the cold has come in quietly and not on a gale. Ink useless below about −40 so the figures are all by hand now. I have started keeping the readings in pencil in a pocket book and copying them up at the stove, and I have lost one day's noon reading that way, which will not happen again. — E.P.B.

**11 July.** Mowbray had Thorsen and me at the table four hours over the depot journey. Where the depot goes, what goes in it, how many days out and back, which dogs. It is nine weeks before anyone can move and we have now planned it three times. Thorsen says the plan will not survive the surface. He is right and it does not stop us. — G.R.M.

**15 July.** Sugar fifty-nine. That is two pounds in a month, which is better than I had feared and worse than it needs to be, and I have written it on the annexe door where the tin sits so that a man taking it can see what he is taking. Mowbray thinks this is a poor way to run a hut. I think a locked box is a poorer one. — H.C.

**19 July.** The depot list is pinned up beside my sugar figure now, which I take as an answer. Forty days of food for three men and two teams, and the whole of it has to come out of what is in the annexe, and nothing in the plan says where the margin comes from. I have asked twice. — H.C.

**20 July.** The dogs are thin in a way I do not like and it is not the food. It is the dark and the standing about. I have started taking them out in it, which the men think is madness because they cannot see, but a dog does not need to see to pull and it needs to pull. — A.T.

**23 July.** Drift so thick at the door that we dug from the inside. Leggat has made a hatch in the roof for exactly this and was insufferable about it for a full day, and had earned it. The dogs were dug out too. One is lame and Thorsen will not say from what. — J.L.

**27 July.** I do not think the men are low. I think they are careful, which looks the same from outside and is not. There is a great deal of politeness in the hut at present and politeness in a hut this size is a thing to watch: it is what men use when they have decided not to say the other thing. — W.S.

**30 July.** Wind dropped in the night and the silence woke four of us. Barometer 29.02. Cloud broke about eleven and there was something over the northern ridge that was not the moon — a paleness, low, perhaps twenty minutes of it. Barrow says nineteen. Nobody went back to bed. — G.R.M.
